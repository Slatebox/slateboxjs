# Slatebox team adoption and declarative dragging

## Goal prompt

Ship Slatebox as the MIT-licensed, open-source Miro replacement where a team can work through a decision together and an enterprise can choose Slatebox Cloud or run the full instance on its own infrastructure. A product owner arriving cold should understand why to bring a colleague, try the differentiator before signing up, and take real work from a shared whiteboard into an interactive decision and a presentation they can reopen.

**Run a release loop.** Inspect the live journey and this roadmap. Rank open gaps by impact on a new team, enterprise adoption and reliability. Give independent work to subagents, choose one observable customer outcome, implement it through normal product entry points, ship, and verify it on the exact deployed build in fresh sessions. Record the revision, environment, journey, evidence, and next gap. Keep looping. A homepage publish, a recorded demo, a green build or one paid-provider success closes only the item it demonstrates. Close the overall goal only when each required journey has evidence or the owner explicitly revises scope. Keep unresolved external dependencies visible and continue independent work.

1. **Make the homepage earn the invitation.** Put intelligence, the MIT-licensed open-source Miro replacement positioning, and a useful first action above the fold. Replace “The last train arrives” and “Meet me at the lift” with reasons a team would switch. For each section, name the decision a colleague faces, show the change someone makes, reveal what it means for the team, and offer the related next action. Use research synthesis, dependency planning, workshops and stakeholder decisions. Let scenarios supply evidence within the demo. Create distinct feature, use-case, developer and enterprise pages with working examples. Follow a new team from landing to first slate, invite, second contributor, presentation and return. Report measured adoption separately from usability evidence.

2. **Show a decision unfolding.** Replace the homepage handoff video player with a native SlateboxJS presentation or an original lightweight Lottie or equivalent inline animation. Show two named teammates flagging a dependency, assigning an owner and agreeing what ships. Use editable native slates where visitors can change the outcome, original choreography for short explanations, and the existing presentation API for actual slides and viewpoints. Make meaningful motion visible early. Respect small screens, keyboard navigation, pausing and reduced motion. Keep the Slatebox mark, use sticky notes and attributed Noun Project shapes, and remove stock imagery, decorative italic headlines and redundant player controls.

   The [Miro enterprise hero](https://miro.com/enterprise/) identifies its media as Lottie. Its [original animation file](https://assets.ctfassets.net/x3tur5ylnyf2/5XuBf37dhO657xWfJghHrp/e85c7ab29920faf735649d2481409ea0/enterprise-JSON.json) contains a vector layer named `SOPHIE` with 36 position keyframes over a 30 fps composition. Use original Slatebox artwork and named cursor choreography for marketing demonstrations. Verify actual remote presence in the app with separate editor sessions.

3. **Make declarative dragging the differentiator.** Incorporate upstream MIT-licensed Dragology into SlateboxJS through a supported, versioned engine API. Retain copyright, license and third-party notices in source and distributed artifacts. Exercise the actual upstream implementation. An author or agent declares a model, how to render it, and which future model states each object can reach. Dragology renders candidate futures to determine where things can go. Compose discrete choices, continuous variations and visual feedback as appropriate. Keep preview separate from committing a valid state. Let the same model drive multiple views which respond together during the gesture. Give ambiguous destinations distinct representations, bound expensive candidate sets and test responsiveness on a representative board. Provide pointer, touch and keyboard access to the same decisions, cancel and undo.

   Use a capacity-limited exhibition as one proof. Hang a piece in a room with one space left. During the drag, a second view previews the visitor’s sightline and shows which pieces can still fit. Cancel restores both views. Commit applies the same decision for a colleague, carries it into the presentation and survives reload. A second proof uses a genuinely different interaction, such as exploring branches with different consequences. Run both from an authored brief and a manually edited model. Have a Pro agent propose a rule change, show its consequences, and let the owner accept or discard it while preserving manual edits. Keep old slates and explicit template links usable as the infographic-first creation path gives way to these experiences. [Research overview](https://declarative-dragging.github.io/) · [Paper](https://declarative-dragging.github.io/D2.pdf) · [Permutation Lab](https://joshuahhh.com/dragology/#/demos/permutation-lab) · [Upstream code and license](https://github.com/declarative-dragging/dragology)

   **Make every advertised gesture a Slatebox capability.** Track the listening room, Moon Gallery, focus darkroom and offline handoff individually in the [engine–app contract](drop-in-contract.md#creative-drag-experiences). For each, inventory what the visitor can change, which other view responds and what persists. Use the packaged engine for those behaviors and the same versioned model in the app. A first-time visitor should be able to open that example as a real slate, try one obvious gesture, see its consequence, rename or change something in place, undo it and try a different outcome. Give the team a working share and presentation path for the result. Keep authoring on the canvas. Test with fresh desktop and narrow-screen sessions, keyboard and touch, including interrupted moves. Record where a person needed a hint. Also author a new two-view experience with a different subject and consequence using the public API. Verify that changing a rule changes where a piece may go and what its audience sees. A collection of fixed illustrations alone still leaves authoring open. Verify every claimed interaction independently. An animated pointer or successful marketing embed proves only the demonstration.

4. **Prove collaboration and presentations across sessions.** Invite a colleague to a private slate. Verify permissions, simultaneous edits and named presence, voice and camera-on playback, leave/rejoin and reconnect. Exercise the shared constrained model with simultaneous moves for the last available slot and verify both clients recover the same legal result. Record results for supported browsers and touch devices. Save and reorder viewpoints on the edited board, present forward and back across distinct views, autoplay where useful, exit to edit, publish, embed and withdraw access. Distinguish local demo participants, staged recordings and real remote-session proofs.

5. **Deliver the deployment and commercial promises.** Verify unlimited individual slates, existing Stripe products, upgrades/downgrades and current subscribers. Resolve the difference between general free AI credits and Pro-only agent tools before making a blanket pricing claim. Verify a clean enterprise deployment through install, invite, collaboration, save, restart, upgrade and restore. Document image architectures, external providers for calls and agents, and how an enterprise gets MIT-licensed source and builds the product. Verify slatebox.com and api.slatebox.com on Cloudflare. Inventory existing Fresh routes and client workflows and demonstrate supported replacements or document deliberate retirements. An embeds health check covers the embed service alone.

6. **Make the app and engine hold up to everyday work.** Carry the marketing design into light-by-default onboarding, dashboard and editor. Check light header/sidebar, dark selected states, cookie notice and unpatterned dark onboarding. Preserve structural libraries. Verify compatible current SVG.js packages and absence of runtime Raphael. Extend existing end-to-end coverage for old boards, icons, custom paths, groups, text and connectors. During repeated resize/rotation, handles track the visible shape, the old outline disappears and the text rotates on every move. Verify save/reload and cross-browser input.

7. **Ship and verify under the existing policy.** Use Roscoe's canonical ship for app and engine, direct to main, with a freshly built local SlateboxJS package and identity stamps. Use the established Cloudflare path for marketing and API. Run targeted preflight and targeted deployed checks, verify bundle identity and release-window logs. Leave the full post-ship suite in the background as instructed and fix regressions forward. Keep PRs and npm publication outside this iteration.

8. **Teach by example.** Refresh docs.slatebox.com with the marketing typography, navigation and palette. Link to it from the developer page. Give the library, app, HTTP API and self-hosting distinct guides. Keep existing reference URLs working. Provide a searchable collection of runnable examples, each with one thing to try, verified code and an observable result.

9. **Keep internal IDs out of default labels.** From a fresh slate, click a connector handle and drag another connector onto empty space. Both gestures should create a connected node ready to name. On the default canvas, selection handles may expose editing controls, but the label must never show generated text such as “Node 351kjiuaq”. Keep the ID in the connection data. Leave both the canvas label and saved text empty until someone names the shape. Screen readers should announce “Unnamed node” before naming and the authored name afterward. Check this in the app editor and on the public documentation canvas. Name it, save, and reopen to verify the authored label and connection. Preserve explicit user-authored names on existing slates. A keyboard user can select a note, choose a connector by direction, create a blank child and name it immediately with F2.

10. **Take a startup team’s question into a real slate.** Give the marketing visitor one editable question and open it in a private slate with the conversation beside the canvas. Use a read-only native preview for illustrations. Put hands-on drawing, drag-and-drop workshops and presentation controls in the product or a deliberately interactive example. A support lead can move a real note, see its attached arrows follow and save a fallback. A founder can try an experiment budget with the library’s capacity rules. A sales engineer can show a design partner where their file goes and correct the map. Verify each advertised gesture, rule and saved result with the app and library, then test its value with actual teams.

11. **Author a map through conversation with Slatebox.** Put the conversation beside the canvas and make it reachable at phone widths. Start on a slate, ask for a path in plain language, try the proposed outcome, keep or discard it, ask a follow-up and undo a change. Explain the available direct-edit commands on free accounts. Use the current stable Vercel AI SDK for paid map chat and preserve the tool calls used by existing assistants. Check entitlements, provider errors, conflicting edits, keyboard access, a read-only share, and the saved result after both editors reopen it.

12. **Let an operator take Slatebox documentation into their own agent.** Every guide needs a readable source, a copyable task with a verifiable outcome, and links an agent can follow without running browser JavaScript. Start with the Docker install. For MCP, CLI, HTTP API and UI parity, exercise the same authorized team journey through each interface. Record capability, permissions, resulting slate and failure behavior for each surface. The existing MCP executable starts a stdio server. A shell user needs independently usable commands for any workflow marked CLI-ready.

13. **Release the open-source product under the Slatebox organization.** Call the maintained self-hosted release Slatebox. Recommend `ghcr.io/slatebox/slatebox` and `ghcr.io/slatebox/relay` for the matched app and collaboration relay. Publish immutable release versions with matched app, engine and relay revisions. The stable `latest` tag should point to the verified community version. Verify both architectures, the Compose install and anonymous pulls from a clean host before advertising registry instructions. The original personal Docker Hub tag last changed in July 2022. The current release kit is distributed on request. Public registry publication is open. ### Evidence for the team value propositions

Use [the Hacker News team concept study](startup-team-concepts-2026-11-18.md) alongside the Miro research. Prefer a real visual editing gesture that a team can recreate in the ordinary editor. Check the marketing rendering surface, authoring path, saved document and second participant before calling a scene a canvas demonstration.

Use Miro as one market reference alongside product discovery practice, observed Slatebox behavior and direct interviews with prospective startup teams. Use each example where it helps the team make a decision.

- **Map dependencies together.** Support and engineering can trace two customer paths through an outage, move a note with its arrows attached and write their fallback on the same diagram. Demonstrate this on the homepage. [Store-level outage report](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50535589)
- **Choose the next customer test.** Research is easier for a designer or engineer to act on when they can see the customer’s original words beside the proposed change. MING Labs describes putting an early prototype in front of customers and recording what happened. Product Talk’s interview snapshots preserve the individual customer story for later comparison. On the research example page, selecting a quote picks one of three prepared screens and saves the associated brief for a teammate. [MING Labs case study](https://miro.com/blog/ming-labs-and-miro/) · [Interview snapshots](https://www.producttalk.org/interview-snapshot/)
- **Try a trade-off before committing the team.** Give the founders one budget and two plausible uses for the same money. While one cofounder moves $200 from newsletter placement to assisted onboarding, the other can see which conversations the team can afford. Preserve their choice when they open the slate. Comparing alternatives and testing assumptions are common discovery practices. [Opportunity solution trees](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/)
- **Present the decision with its evidence.** Munich Re describes rebuilding the story across documents before a stakeholder review. Demonstrate a Slatebox presentation that follows the customer file, the missing owners and the launch decision, using the notes the team just edited. A working presentation verifies the product capability. Observe whether a startup chooses it for its own review before making an adoption or time-saving claim. [Munich Re case study](https://miro.com/blog/munich-re-builds-customer-centric-products-with-miro/)
